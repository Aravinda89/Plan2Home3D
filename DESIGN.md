# Sisila House: design brief

*Concept design · 15 Oct 2026 · Drawing A-101 · Plan2Home3D Studio*

> **Sisila** (සිසිල) means coolness, a cool breeze, in Sinhala. That is the main thing this house is designed to give.

## The brief
A home for one or two people who work in AI and technology, paint and photograph, and want to live close to nature. It should be minimalist, simple and elegant, and just big enough for daily life, with a guest for a few nights now and then. It has to handle Sri Lanka's heat, humidity and two monsoons without depending on air-conditioning.

## Concept
Tropical modernism in the spirit of Geoffrey Bawa: **two long pavilions** under deep clay-tile hip roofs, joined by a flat-roofed link around an **open-to-sky courtyard** with a frangipani (araliya) tree and a pond. The bedroom, dining room, gallery and bath all look into the courtyard. Rain chains carry monsoon water from the eaves into the pond.

The plan is organised by daylight:

- **South pavilion:** living, kitchen and master bedroom, all opening onto a 2.4 m verandah and the garden.
- **North pavilion:** the studio, edit suite and work room, which get the even, glare-free north light that painters and photographers need.
- **West edge:** a buffer of baths, utility and storage, so few windows face the afternoon sun.
- **East:** the entrance, porch and carport facing the road and the morning sun.

## Climate strategy
| Challenge | Response |
|---|---|
| **Monsoon rain** | Clay tiles at 25°. Eaves of 1.2–1.5 m keep walls dry and windows open in storms. Gutters and rain chains feed the pond and a 5,000 L tank. |
| **Heat** | Long axis east–west. Big glazing only behind the verandah and courtyard. The west wall is solid and lined with service rooms. |
| **Humidity** | Openings on two sides of every room, timber louvres with mesh and vented eaves. The courtyard pulls warm air up and out. |
| **Flood, damp, termites** | A 450 mm granite rubble plinth with a damp-proof course and a termite barrier. |
| **Energy** | About 4 kWp of solar PV and a solar water heater on the south roof, a solar pergola and EV point at the carport. |

The 3D model's sun-path slider uses the real solar geometry for latitude 7° N. From about April to August the sun is in the northern sky, and from October to February in the southern sky. The deep eaves shade the walls in both seasons.

## Materials
Lime plaster (off-white) · half-round clay roof tiles · laterite garden walls · polished cement floors · terracotta verandah tiles · jak and teak joinery · granite plinth · water.

## Landscape
A Na tree (*Mesua ferrea*, the national tree) shades the entrance. A kumbuk tree shades the south-west, king coconuts stand at the corners, bamboo screens the north boundary and the courtyard has an araliya. A gravel service yard on the west holds the rainwater tank and the drying area.

## Assumptions
- The plot size, orientation and wet-zone location are assumed. The layout mirrors or rotates to suit a real site.
- The kitchen in the south-east, master bedroom in the south-west and open centre are also consistent with traditional Vastu.
